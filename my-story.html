I was born and raised in Taipei, Taiwan, a vibrant city that shaped my curiosity about the world around me.

At National Chengchi University (NCCU), I pursued a dual degree in Statistics and Geomatics, building a foundation in data analysis and spatial science.

In summer 2022, I began my first internship as a Spatial Analysis Intern at Academia Sinica.

I analyzed temperature changes and future land-use scenarios to explore the challenges and opportunities facing Taiwan’s agricultural land under two Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs).

In summer 2023, I joined the National Science and Technology Center for Disaster Reduction as a Drone Mapping Intern.

Working with my team, I contributed to UAV mapping of landslide areas, from flight planning and ground control point setup to 3D modeling and landslide volume estimation using time-series orthophotos and digital surface models.

Later in 2023, I joined Professor Shih-Yuan Lin’s research group at NCCU, where I explored ESG-related research and remote sensing, collected and organized research materials, and processed Sentinel-2 imagery.

From fall 2023 to spring 2024, I conducted an independent study on detecting spatiotemporal changes in ponds across Taoyuan, Taiwan, using satellite image time series, supported by an undergraduate research fellowship.

During this period, I also served as a teaching assistant for Aerial Photogrammetry.

In February 2024, I graduated from NCCU with a BSc in Statistics and a BA in Geomatics.

Before starting my master’s degree, I spent five months as a Project Management Intern at Strong Engineering Consulting.

I analyzed more than 100 gravel pits on public land using UAV imagery and 3D spatial data, developed technical training materials, and automated workflows using Google Apps Script, AppSheet, and Python.

In fall 2024, I began the International Cartography MSc programme, with lectures delivered by Technical Universities of Munich, Vienna, and Dresden, alongside 30 classmates from 23 other countries.

One of the highlights was living in a different city each semester, and experiencing new cultures and perspectives while deepening my understanding of spatial analysis and visual design in cartography.

In April 2026, I returned to Vienna to complete my Master’s thesis at VRVis GmbH.

I designed and developed an interactive 3D prototype to visualize urban thermal stress at the neighborhood scale, then evaluated its usability through exploratory tasks, questionnaires, and interviews. After successfully defending my thesis, I graduated with an MSc in Cartography.

Currently, I work as a freelance cartographer and data visualizer while seeking full-time opportunities. I enjoy transforming complex spatial data into clear, engaging, and meaningful visual experiences.

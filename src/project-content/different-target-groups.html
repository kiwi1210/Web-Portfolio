Similar Topics, Different Target Groups

This project explores how the same urban topic, mobility in Taipei, can be communicated differently depending on the target audience and intended message. Two persuasive maps were designed to present contrasting perspectives on everyday mobility - one highlighting the efficiency and connectivity of the city’s transportation system, and the other revealing the potential risks pedestrians may face in daily traffic environments.

Taipei in Motion

The first map targets Taipei citizens and tourists who appreciate the convenience of the city’s public transportation system. It emphasizes the accessibility and connectivity of Taipei’s multi-mobility network by visualizing metro lines, bus routes, YouBike rental stations, and the street network. These feature layers are stacked with subtle shading effects and displayed in an oblique perspective, creating a layered and slightly personal viewing angle rather than a purely objective top-down representation.

The visual design uses cool tones including white, black, and gray, to create a modern and reliable aesthetic. Bright, vibrant colors highlight key transportation layers, symbolizing efficiency, energy, and the density of the network. A marble-textured background reinforces the impression of a contemporary and structured urban environment. Designed in an A3 poster format, the map is intended to attract attention in public spaces and promote a positive image of Taipei as a city with highly efficient and well-connected mobility infrastructure.

Yi-Ting's Walk Home Shouldn't be This Dangerous

The second map focuses on Taipei citizens, particularly pedestrians who may feel vulnerable in the city’s traffic environment. Instead of emphasizing infrastructure efficiency, this map highlights the hidden risks embedded in everyday mobility. It visualizes the walking route of a virtual character named Yi-Ting as she travels home through areas with high accident density. Along her path, the map points out potential hazards such as heavy traffic loads, obstructed sightlines at intersections, and unsafe crossings.

The design intentionally uses dominant red tones to evoke feelings of danger, warning, and insecurity. The walking route is represented with a black-and-white slash pattern resembling a running computer program, suggesting that this journey is happening in real time and that someone could be walking this path at this very moment. In the lower-left corner, a small inset map uses a skin-toned texture as the base map with red thematic overlays, symbolizing bleeding and emphasizing the physical consequences of traffic accidents. The concluding paragraph in the lower-right corner is written in all capital letters to create a strong warning or “screaming” visual effect.

The map is designed in a flyer format (A4 or A5), allowing it to be easily distributed or posted in various public locations. Its primary goal is to raise awareness of traffic safety issues and encourage reflection on pedestrian vulnerability in everyday urban life.
